For parents

A jargon-free guide for parents.

This isn't for the cubers — it's for you. If your child wants to compete and you have no idea what any of it means, start here.

Before the competition

Is there an age limit?

No — there's no minimum or maximum age, and no separate categories by age. A seven-year-old and a fifty-year-old compete in exactly the same round, under the same rules.

What are all these different events?

Beyond the classic 3x3 cube, the WCA recognizes 17 official events — things like 2x2, solving blindfolded, or solving one-handed. Most local competitions only run a handful of these in a day. Our Learn page is the place to start with 3x3 itself.

Does my child need to be fast to enter?

Not at all. There's no qualifying time for most competitions — first-timers regularly compete in the same room as national record holders.

Can we just come and watch first?

Yes. Spectating is usually free, and it's a great way to see what a competition actually feels like before signing up to compete.

How do we find out about competitions?

Check our Competitions page, or follow @cubing.am — every new competition is announced there as soon as it's approved by the WCA.

How does registration work?

Your child will need a free WCA account, then you register for the specific competition through the official WCA website. Spots can be limited and registration usually opens weeks ahead, so don't wait too long once one's announced.

What to bring

What do we need to bring on the day?

Formal identification — a passport, ID card, or birth certificate — so a WCA ID can be issued if this is your child's first competition. Also bring their own cube(s) for whichever events they've registered for.

If plans change

Can we get a refund if we can't make it?

Usually only if you cancel before the deadline stated on that competition's page — after that, refunds typically aren't possible, since the organizer has already committed to venue costs.

My child doesn't want to compete anymore — what do we do?

Completely normal, especially before a first competition. Just message the organizers via @cubing.am as early as you can, so someone on the waiting list can take the spot.

On the day

What actually happens at a competition?

You check in at the registration desk with ID, then wait to be called up in a group. When it's your child's turn, they get up to 15 seconds to inspect the scrambled cube, then start the timer and solve. A judge watches the whole thing and records the result.

Is my child safe? Can I leave them on their own?

Every competition has a WCA Delegate overseeing it, plus a judge at every table. That said, especially for a first competition, most parents choose to stay nearby — competitions typically run a few hours in one room.

How do we know how they did?

Results are entered live during the competition on WCA Live, so you can watch times come in as they happen, right from your phone.

Glossary — what do all these words mean?

WCA
World Cube Association — the global body that governs official competitions.
Delegate
A WCA-certified volunteer who makes sure a competition follows the rules.
DNF
"Did Not Finish" — recorded when a solve isn't completed validly.
Cutoff
A time limit in early solves that decides whether a competitor gets further attempts.
Scramble
The specific random mixed-up state every competitor in a round solves from.
Judge
The volunteer who times and verifies each individual solve.
